# Phishing Awareness Guide

## Introduction

Phishing is a cyberattack in which criminals impersonate trusted organisations or individuals to trick people into revealing confidential information, clicking malicious links or opening harmful attachments. Phishing attacks can arrive through email, text messages, social media and other communication platforms.

## Common Warning Signs

1. **Suspicious links:** The link may lead to a fake website.
2. **Urgent messages:** Attackers may threaten to suspend an account unless immediate action is taken.
3. **Unexpected attachments:** Files may contain malware.
4. **Requests for sensitive information:** Messages may ask for passwords or verification codes.
5. **Unusual sender addresses:** The address may imitate a legitimate organisation.
6. **Unexpected payment requests:** Criminals may impersonate managers or suppliers.

## How to Protect Yourself

- Verify the sender using a trusted communication channel.
- Avoid clicking unexpected links or opening suspicious attachments.
- Visit official websites by entering their addresses yourself.
- Use strong, unique passwords.
- Enable multifactor authentication.
- Keep your operating system and applications updated.
- Report suspicious messages to your IT or cybersecurity team.

## What to Do If You Receive a Suspicious Email

1. Do not click links or open attachments.
2. Do not reply or provide personal information.
3. Report the message using your organisation's approved procedure.
4. If you clicked a suspicious link, inform your IT security team promptly.
5. Follow the security team's instructions to protect affected accounts or devices.

## Conclusion

Cybersecurity awareness helps reduce the risk of phishing attacks. Always verify unexpected requests and report suspicious activity promptly.
